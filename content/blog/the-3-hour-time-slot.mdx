When you plan a week in OnPacts, you choose two things: how many days you'll work out, and a **3-hour time slot** for those sessions. The first one feels obvious. The second one sometimes raises an eyebrow. Why does it matter *when* you work out, as long as you do?

Because "sometime today" is where good intentions go to disappear.

## The problem with "sometime today"

If your plan is to exercise at some point during the day, every hour gives you a fresh chance to postpone. Morning becomes "after lunch", lunch becomes "this evening", and the evening becomes "tomorrow, definitely".

Nothing dramatic happens. The day just fills up. By the time you think about your workout again, there's no room left.

A time slot closes that gap. Instead of an open-ended to-do, your workout has an address in your day.

## Why a slot builds habits

Habits form when an action is repeated in a consistent context. The same time, the same cues, the same sequence. Brushing your teeth doesn't take willpower because it's anchored to waking up and going to bed.

A time slot gives your workout that same kind of anchor. After a few weeks of walking between 6 and 9 PM, the evening itself starts to feel like walking time. Fewer decisions, less negotiation, more doing.

## Why three hours?

Three hours is a balance between structure and flexibility.

- It's **narrow enough** to create a real routine. You know roughly when the session will happen.
- It's **wide enough** to absorb normal life. A meeting runs over, dinner is late, the bus is slow, and you can still make it.

A one-hour window would be fragile. A whole day would be meaningless. Three hours gives your plan room to breathe without letting it drift.

## How the slot works

The rules are straightforward:

- A completed activity **counts toward your weekly goal**, whenever you check out.
- To **earn a reward** for that day, you need to **check out inside your time slot**.
- Checking out outside the slot still counts toward the goal, but **isn't rewarded**.

So the slot doesn't punish you for a late session. Your week still stays on track. It simply reserves rewards for the days you kept the whole promise, including the when.

## Choosing your slot

The right slot is the one that matches the life you actually have, not the life you wish you had. A few questions can help.

### When are you most reliably free?

Not when you're *occasionally* free, but most days. For many people that's early morning, before the household wakes, or the evening, after work.

### When do you have the most energy?

Some people love a dawn walk; others can barely find their shoes before 8 AM. Pick a window that works with your natural rhythm, not against it.

### What usually gets in the way?

If evenings often get swallowed by work or family, a morning slot may protect your sessions better. If mornings are a scramble, try lunchtime or after dinner.

### Can you finish comfortably inside it?

Remember that you need to check out inside the window to be rewarded. If your typical session takes 45 minutes, start early enough in the slot to finish with time to spare.

## Small tricks that help

- **Set the cue, not just the time.** "When I close my laptop, I put on my shoes" is stronger than "sometime after six".
- **Prepare in advance.** Shoes by the door, a charged phone, a route in mind.
- **Start early in the slot.** Treat the first hour as the real target and the rest as a buffer.
- **Use the reminders.** OnPacts can notify you about your plan and time slot, so it doesn't sneak past you.

## Adjusting over time

Your first slot is an experiment, not a life sentence. If it isn't working, change it for the following week before the Sunday 11:55 PM deadline. Seasons change, jobs change, routines change. Your slot can change with them.

What matters is that each week you choose a slot on purpose, then keep it. Do that for long enough, and you'll find you don't need to think about your workout much at all. It'll just be what you do at that time of day.
