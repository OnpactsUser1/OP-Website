Many fitness apps have a leaderboard. Someone is first, someone is last, and everyone in between can see exactly where they stand. For a small group of very competitive people, that's motivating. For most of us, it's quietly discouraging.

OnPacts doesn't have one, and that's a deliberate choice. Instead, we talk about **discipline**: a personal measure of how well you keep the commitments you made to yourself.

## Discipline, defined simply

In OnPacts, discipline isn't about intensity, speed or how many steps you can rack up. It's about the gap between two things:

1. **What you said you'd do**: the days you committed to and the time slot you chose.
2. **What you actually did**: the sensor-verified activities you completed and when you checked out.

The smaller that gap, week after week, the stronger your discipline. That's it. A person who commits to three days and completes all three is more disciplined, in OnPacts terms, than someone who commits to six and completes four.

## What shapes your discipline score

Your discipline score and streaks reflect your consistency over time. The things that matter are the things you control:

- **Keeping committed days** with completed activities.
- **Checking out inside your time slot**, so the when matches the plan.
- **Completing whole weeks**, not just individual days.
- **Coming back after a missed week**, rather than drifting away.

Your **Weekly History** shows each week as **Reward Earned** or **Rewards Missed**, so you can see your pattern at a glance. Over time, those weeks tell a story, and it's yours alone.

## Why we left the leaderboard out

### Comparison measures the wrong thing

A leaderboard compares people with completely different lives. A parent of three fitting a walk into a hectic evening and a student with free afternoons are not running the same race. Ranking them against each other says nothing useful about either.

Discipline compares you with **you**: your plan and your follow-through. That's a fair fight.

### Competition can push people the wrong way

When the goal is to beat others, people tend to overcommit, skip rest, or chase numbers that don't fit their lives. OnPacts is meant to build steady habits, not to encourage anyone to push past what's sensible for them. Consistency over intensity, every time.

### Rewards should be about effort, not rank

OnPacts rewards are tied only to what you did. There's no prize for coming first, no luck, and nothing that depends on someone else doing worse. If every member kept every commitment, every one of them would be rewarded. That's what makes it fair, and it's a big part of why OnPacts is not a contest and not gambling.

## Community without competition

Leaving out a leaderboard doesn't mean going it alone. OnPacts lets you make **community connections**, people who can encourage you, celebrate a kept week with you, and nudge you when you're wobbling.

Encouragement works best when nobody is keeping score against you. You can cheer someone on without worrying about where you rank, and they can do the same for you.

## Honest by design

A discipline score is only meaningful if the data behind it is real. That's why OnPacts only counts sensor-verified activity, requires automatic date and time settings, and blocks rooted phones. These rules aren't about suspicion. They're what make your score something you can trust, and something you can be proud of.

## What a disciplined week feels like

It's rarely dramatic. It looks like a plan made calmly on Sunday, a walk taken on a Wednesday when you'd rather have stayed in, a leave used honestly on the day you were unwell, and a checkout inside your slot on a busy Friday.

None of those moments would put you at the top of a leaderboard. All of them add up to something better: becoming the kind of person who does what they said they'd do.

That's what discipline means in OnPacts. Not beating anyone else. Just showing up for the pact you made.
