When you start an activity in OnPacts, you choose between two kinds of challenge: **steps** or **calories**. Both count toward your weekly goal, both are tracked and verified by your phone, and both can earn rewards when you check out inside your time slot.

So which one should you pick? The honest answer is: the one you're most likely to complete on a normal day. Let's look at each.

## The steps challenge

A steps session is exactly what it sounds like. You walk or run, and OnPacts counts your steps toward a target.

During the session, the live workout screen shows your steps taken, a sensor confidence score, your target, and how much time has passed. Your phone's step counter, accelerometer and, during the workout, GPS work together to make sure the movement is real.

### Steps suit you if…

- **You like to get outside.** A walk around the neighbourhood, a loop in the park or a run along a familiar route all fit naturally.
- **You want something simple.** There's nothing to set up. Put on your shoes and go.
- **Your days are unpredictable.** A brisk walk can happen almost anywhere: before work, at lunch, after dinner.
- **You're building a habit from scratch.** Walking is gentle, familiar and easy to repeat, which is exactly what a new habit needs.

### A tip for steps

Choose a target you can finish comfortably inside your time slot on an ordinary day. If you regularly hit it with time to spare, raise it gradually. Completing the session matters more than how big the number is.

## The calories challenge

A calories session is built for training: the kind of workout where you might not cover much ground, but you're definitely working. OnPacts tracks your activity with your phone's sensors and estimates the calories toward your goal.

### Calories suit you if…

- **You train indoors.** Home workouts, a gym session or a class can all fit a calories challenge.
- **You enjoy variety.** Mixing different kinds of training keeps things interesting.
- **Steps don't reflect your effort.** If your workout is intense but stationary, a calories goal can be a better fit than counting steps.

### A tip for calories

Keep your phone on you, in a pocket or an armband, so its sensors can follow your movement. Activities are verified by the phone, so a phone left on a bench can't see the work you're doing.

## A note on numbers

Calorie figures from any phone or wearable are estimates. They're useful for tracking effort over time and comparing one week with the next, but they aren't precise measurements, and they aren't medical information. Treat the number as a consistent yardstick, not a verdict on your body.

The same goes for steps. A few hundred steps of difference between devices is normal. What matters is that you showed up and did the work.

## You don't have to choose forever

Your choice isn't permanent. You pick the type when you start an activity, so you can match it to the day:

- Walk on days when the weather's good and you want fresh air.
- Train on days when you're short on time or stuck indoors.

Many members settle into a rhythm, such as steps on weekdays and a calories session at the weekend. Others stick with one type because the routine itself is what keeps them going. Both approaches work.

## What both challenges have in common

Whichever you choose, the rules are the same:

- **Your phone's sensors track and verify the activity.** Only sensor-verified activity counts.
- **Tracking continues with the screen off**, so you can lock your phone and focus.
- **A completed activity counts toward your weekly goal.**
- **To earn a reward for that day, check out inside your time slot.**

If the app gets interrupted, come back within 5 minutes to continue. Otherwise, the activity is checked out automatically and sent when your phone is online.

## So, which one?

If you're not sure, start with steps. They're simple, flexible and kind to a brand-new habit. Once your weeks feel steady, try a calories session and see how it fits.

The best challenge isn't the hardest one. It's the one you'll still be doing in ten weeks' time.
