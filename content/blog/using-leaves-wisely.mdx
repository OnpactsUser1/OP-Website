You plan your week carefully. You pick a sensible number of days and a time slot that fits. Then, on Thursday, your child wakes up with a fever, or a work deadline swallows the evening, or you simply feel unwell.

A commitment app that ignored days like that would be unrealistic, and a bit unkind. That's why OnPacts gives you **leaves**.

## How leaves work

The rules are short:

- You get **2 leaves per month**.
- A leave can be used **only during an active week**, for a committed day whose goal is **still open**.
- Each leave asks for a short reason, **100 to 300 words**.
- A leave **counts toward your committed days**, so your week stays intact.
- A leave day **earns no reward**.
- Leaves **reset on the 1st** of each month and **don't roll over**.

In other words, a leave protects your week without pretending you worked out. It's an honest pause, not a loophole.

## Why leaves exist

OnPacts is built on a simple idea: you're rewarded for what you actually did. A week only earns a reward if every committed day is fulfilled. Without leaves, one bad day would wipe out an otherwise excellent week.

That would push people towards two unhelpful habits: committing to fewer days than they could really manage, just to be safe, or giving up on the rest of the week once a day was lost. Leaves remove that pressure. You can commit with confidence, knowing there's a fair way to handle the unexpected.

## Why a leave earns nothing

It might seem strict that a leave day doesn't earn a reward. But think about what rewards are for: the days you showed up inside your slot and did the work. A leave keeps your week alive so you can still be rewarded for the days you *did* complete. It just doesn't pay for the day you didn't.

That keeps everything honest, for you and for everyone else using OnPacts.

## Why the reason matters

Writing 100 to 300 words might feel like a lot when you're tired. That's partly the point. The reason gives you a moment to pause and check in: is this a genuine need, or a moment of "I don't feel like it"?

Often, writing it out confirms that you need the rest. Sometimes, halfway through, you realise you could manage a short walk after all. Either outcome is a good one. The reason also leaves a record you can look back on, so you can spot patterns, such as Thursdays that always get crowded.

## When to use a leave

There's no perfect rule, but these are good reasons:

- **You're unwell.** Rest. Your body is telling you something, and no streak is worth ignoring it.
- **A genuine emergency or family need.** Look after the people who need you.
- **Unavoidable travel or a schedule clash** you couldn't have planned for on Sunday.

And a few moments when it might be worth pausing first:

- **You're just not in the mood.** Try starting a short session first. Momentum often appears once you're moving.
- **It's early in the month** and you've got a busy few weeks ahead. You only have two, so consider whether you'll need them more later.

## Leaves vs. breaks

Leaves and breaks solve different problems:

- A **leave** covers a single committed day during an active week.
- A **break week** is planned before the week begins, by Sunday 11:55 PM, and has no commitment at all: no gain and no loss.

If you already know a whole week will be chaotic, such as holidays, a house move or exams, plan a break instead of hoping two leaves will stretch far enough.

## Let go of the guilt

Using a leave isn't failing. It's the system working as designed. The people who build lasting habits aren't the ones who never miss a day; they're the ones who handle the missed days well and come straight back.

So use your leaves when you need them, write an honest reason, and pick up where you left off tomorrow. On the 1st, you'll have two fresh ones waiting.
